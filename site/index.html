Thin reserves don't reliably predict a bailout

Latest-year (2024–25) unallocated reserves as a share of net revenue expenditure, all 17 authorities, thinnest first. Colour marks the risk band.

Trafford is the only Greater Manchester or Gloucestershire authority that needed a 2025–26 Exceptional Financial Support award (a capitalisation direction, £9.6m) — but it isn't the authority with the thinnest reserves. Manchester's unallocated reserves have run thinner than Trafford's for most of the last 8 years, and its 2024–25 reading (1.3% of net spend) is the second-lowest of any authority in this scope — behind only Gloucester City's one-year collapse to 0.7% — yet neither Manchester nor Gloucester has needed one.

Read plainly: a thin reserves ratio is necessary context for financial stress, not a sufficient predictor of it. Two authorities are currently thinner than the one that actually needed help. Whatever is letting Manchester and Gloucester run leaner than Trafford without triggering the same intervention — commercial income, borrowing headroom, MRP policy — isn't visible in this dataset. That's the open question this project doesn't yet answer.

Methodology & caveats
  • What's measured: unallocated (general fund) reserves at year-end, as a percentage of net revenue expenditure that year. Unallocated reserves are what a council can actually spend without breaching earmarking — total reserves (including schools, public health, earmarked funds) would overstate what's really available.
  • Risk band is a transparent rule, not a fitted model: High = an actual Section 114 notice or 2025–26 Exceptional Financial Support award; Elevated = latest year's ratio has collapsed below 3% regardless of history, or the 8-year average itself is under 5%; Watch = 8-year average 5–8%, or the latest year is under half the 8-year average; Lower risk = none of the above — still just a reserves-based signal, not a clean bill of health.
  • The thresholds are a first-pass judgement call, not statistically validated. They were adjusted once already: an earlier version banded Gloucester as "lower risk" on its comfortable 8-year average alone, missing that its latest year had collapsed to 0.7%.
  • Small district councils are noisier. Cotswold's ratio swings from 5% to 88% across the 8 years, purely because its net revenue expenditure denominator is small — don't read district-vs-borough gaps as apples-to-apples without accounting for this.
  • Two-tier vs unitary: Greater Manchester's 10 boroughs are unitary authorities running their own social care and highways budgets. Gloucestershire is two-tier — the county council runs those services, the 6 districts don't — so reserves pressure shows up differently between the two groups.
  • Exceptional Financial Support and Section 114 reference data were compiled via automated fetches from gov.uk and secondary sources, not hand-verified line by line against primary documents. Treat as indicative pending a manual check.
  • This is a screening tool, not a forecast. Reserves-to-expenditure is one input among several a body like CIPFA would use in a proper resilience assessment (borrowing levels and capital financing requirement aren't in this dataset yet).
